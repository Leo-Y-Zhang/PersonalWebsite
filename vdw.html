Van der Waerden numbers

A reference for five mixed families, with certificates and proof provenance.

What this page is

For years the community’s reference tables for van der Waerden data were maintained by Tanbir Ahmed, whose computations produced many of the values below; his pages have since gone offline. This page is a verifiable successor for the five mixed families I work on: every term the OEIS records, credited as the OEIS credits it; every term I added with a certificate you can check yourself; and every claim labelled by the kind of evidence behind it.

  • Lower bounds are certificates. Each term I added links a witness colouring; checking it needs the definition and nothing else.
  • Upper bounds are labelled by their evidence. The refutations behind those five terms have been reduced to formally checked proof objects: 23,851 per-cube DRAT proofs across the five families, each replayed to s VERIFIED by drat-trim, plus a checked proof for each family that its cube set is exhaustive.

Each term is credited as its OEIS entry credits it: the entry’s author for its original terms, and the EXTENSIONS line for each term added later. None of the five terms I added was a first determination. Marek Ochocki’s preprint of June 2026 has four of them, and S. Karki published A217005 a(19) = 52 in 2024. What MathRecords adds for those five is a second derivation by a different method, with each upper bound reduced to checked DRAT proofs.

The tables are generated from the OEIS and cross-checked against the b-files in MathRecords by a script that refuses to build the page if the two sources disagree. It was last regenerated 24 November 2026.

Verify it yourself

The witness colourings check in milliseconds with verify_certificate.py. In CI, verify_all.py re-checks every claim in the repository against the evidence committed beside it. DRAT.md documents the DRAT machinery, including the measured cost of each proof and why colour symmetry had to come out of the cube set before the equal-target families could be certified. Terms credited to other people are shown as the OEIS records them; MathRecords does not re-derive them.
